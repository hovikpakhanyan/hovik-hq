## Allocation: 60 / 40

- **60% core sleeve.** Well-performing quality names: established, liquid companies and broad funds meant to be held for years. This is the foundation of the account.
- **40% high-upside / higher-risk sleeve.** Asymmetric bets, where the potential upside is large relative to the amount risked. This sleeve can lose money, including most or all of a position.

This is a target mix, not a daily mandate. The account will not sit at exactly 60/40 every day. Suggested default classifications (to be confirmed as holdings are reviewed): broad funds such as VOO, VYM, and QQQ, and quality large-caps, lean core; leveraged funds such as TQQQ, and speculative names such as RGTI, lean to the 40% sleeve. Overlap matters: QQQ and TQQQ already hold names such as NVDA, AMD, and GOOGL, so those bets can stack.

## Active trading (light, on top of the core)

The goal is to "move things around" a few times a month, not to day-trade.

- **Where it lives:** inside the 40% upside sleeve. A suggested ceiling is about 10% of the total account for active swing trades; the rest of the 40% holds longer-term upside bets.
- **Pace:** swing trades held for days to weeks. Roughly one to four new trades a month; no daily trading quota.
- **Every trade gets a written plan first:** why, entry, profit target, and the price where it's wrong (exit). The morning scan's upside-fit alerts are the idea source.
- **Risk per trade:** keep any single trade's planned loss small, about 1% of the total account, so one bad call never dents the $5M path.
- **No margin and no options.** Leverage stays limited to what's already in TQQQ.
- **Profits from wins** can be swept into the 60% core at the quarterly check, which is how active trading feeds long-term wealth.
- **Taxes:** gains on positions held under a year are taxed as ordinary income, so frequent trading in a taxable account costs more. Trading inside a retirement account, if available, avoids that; confirm with a tax pro.
- **Rehearse first:** new setups go through paper trading before real money.
- **Monthly review:** log wins, losses, and whether each trade followed its plan. If active trading lags simply holding VOO over two quarters, shrink it.

## Wealth tips (HQ column)

A running column of practical tips on the Markets page. These are general education, not personalized tax or legal advice; confirm specifics with a tax pro.

- **Trade inside a retirement account when possible.** In an IRA or Roth IRA, trades don't trigger yearly taxes, so active trading keeps more of its gains.
- **Roth IRA first for long-term money.** Growth and qualified withdrawals are tax-free, which matters a lot on a long path to $5M. Check the current year's contribution limit and income rules.
- **Grab any employer 401(k) match.** It's an instant return before the market does anything.
- **Automate something, even small.** A small automatic deposit keeps the habit going in lean months; add extra in big months.
- **Reinvest dividends** (VYM, VOO and others) so they compound.
- **Keep an emergency fund outside the account** (SPAXX or savings), so you never have to sell stocks at a bad time.
- **Use losses wisely.** In a taxable account, selling a loser can offset gains (tax-loss harvesting); watch the wash-sale rule.
- **Hold winners over a year when it makes sense.** Long-term gains are taxed at lower rates than short-term ones.
- **Keep fees low.** Low-cost index funds keep more money compounding.
- **For the kids:** look into 529 plans for education and a custodial Roth IRA once a child has earned income.

Each alert is tagged against this 60-40 plan:

- **Core fit** — belongs in the 60% quality sleeve
- **Upside fit** — belongs in the 40% high-upside sleeve (including paper-trade ideas)
- **Noise** — worth knowing, but not an action for either sleeve

Reports are a fast rundown: one bold headline takeaway per story, one short line of context, and a source link for more detail.

These weekday research alerts are delivered in the Grok Bot stock-research chat only, and only when something materially new matters. They are not auto-published elsewhere until a secure publishing path is in place.
